Nobody is watching, and everybody assumes somebody is.

The failure is always the same shape. A vendor posts a deprecation notice in March. Nobody on the team is subscribed. In October a request starts returning 410 Gone, at 3am, in the part of the system that takes money.

The tools you already run cannot see it coming. Your lockfile scanner watches versions you chose; this is a change the vendor makes on their side, to code you never had. Status pages describe the present tense. A page-change monitor fires on every marketing tweak and knows nothing about your repository.

Two halves nobody had joined.

Reasonable questions

Does my source code leave my machine?

No. The scanner reads the repository and stores short snippets as evidence so a finding can point at a line. Self-hosted, nothing leaves at all.

What happens when a vendor moves their changelog?

A built-in check reports every dead feed, and repairing them is the standing maintenance job. A silent blind spot would be the one unforgivable failure, so source health is a first-class screen.

Will it drown me?

The severity model exists to prevent exactly that. Announcements are scored, "no action required" is weighted down, old news only resurfaces if it still matches your code, and a newly connected Slack channel never replays the backlog.

What if the scanner cannot see a call?

Then it says so. A dynamically built URL may not be detected, and a finding with no code match is labelled "no direct match" rather than dressed up as certainty.
